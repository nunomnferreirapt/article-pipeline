# Claims

Merged from the three research files. Duplicates removed; where two sources
made the same point, the one with the figure was kept and the other noted.

## C1
- Claim: The survey covered 140 small crews, surveyed between March and June 2025.
- Source: [S1]
- Quote: "Between March and June 2025 we surveyed 140 small field crews."

## C2
- Claim: A small crew means two to six people on site.
- Source: [S1]
- Quote: "A crew counted as small when it had two to six people on site."

## C3
- Claim: 87 of 140 crews are offline for at least an hour on a typical day.
- Source: [S1]
- Quote: "Of the 140 crews surveyed, 87 reported at least one offline hour on a typical working day."

## C4
- Claim: The median crew is offline three hours a day.
- Source: [S1]
- Quote: "The median crew spent 3 hours a day without a usable connection."

## C5
- Claim: Offline-first means the device stores every record first and syncs later.
- Source: [S1]
- Quote: "stores every record on the device first and syncs when a connection returns"

## C6
- Claim: End-of-day sync fell from 3.5 hours to 40 minutes on average.
- Source: [S1]
- Quote: "Crews that moved to offline-first tools cut their end-of-day sync time from 3.5 hours to 40 minutes on average."

## C7
- Claim: Before the switch the main evening task was re-entering paper notes.
- Source: [S1]
- Quote: "Before the switch, the most common evening task was re-entering notes taken on paper during the offline hours."

## C8
- Claim: One crew lost about two hours every evening to re-entry.
- Source: [S2]
- Quote: "We lost about two hours every evening to re-entering what we had already written down once."

## C9
- Claim: The motive was time back, not technology.
- Source: [S2]
- Quote: "Nobody on the crew asked for new technology. We wanted our evenings back."

## C10
- Claim: The crew's test for a field tool is that it works with no signal at all.
- Source: [S2]
- Quote: "The app has to work in a hole in the ground or it is not a field tool."

## C11
- Claim: Adoption took about two weeks; trust in the offline save was the hurdle.
- Source: [S2]
- Quote: "The first week was rough because people did not trust that the data was saved when the screen said offline."

## C12
- Claim: 62 percent of 2024 adopters still used the tool a year later.
- Source: [S1]
- Quote: "Of the crews that adopted an offline-first tool in 2024, 62 percent were still using it a year later."

## C13
- Claim: Crews that abandoned it cited sync conflicts, stale reference data and missing audit trails.
- Source: [S1]
- Quote: "The reasons given most often were sync conflicts between devices, stale reference data, and a lack of an audit trail that their client would accept."

## C14
- Claim: Sync conflicts are the normal case for field crews.
- Source: [S3]
- Quote: "Sync conflicts are not an edge case for field crews. They are the normal case whenever two people measure the same thing on the same day."

## C15
- Claim: In a small team conflicts get resolved by whoever syncs last.
- Source: [S3]
- Quote: "A small team has nobody whose job it is to resolve those conflicts, so they get resolved by whoever happens to sync last."

## C16
- Claim: The crew lead's first conflict screen cost a morning.
- Source: [S2]
- Quote: "The first time two devices edited the same record we got a conflict screen that nobody understood."

## C17
- Claim: Stale reference data is invisible to the app and the user.
- Source: [S3]
- Quote: "The app does not know the list is old, and neither does the person using it."

## C18
- Claim: The crew now syncs every device before leaving the depot.
- Source: [S2]
- Quote: "Now the rule is that the lead syncs every device before we leave the depot."

## C19
- Claim: Audit trails have a blind spot before sync; offline-first is not free in regulated settings.
- Source: [S3]
- Quote: "The hours before sync are a blind spot. Offline-first is not a free lunch in regulated settings."

## C20
- Claim: Small teams rarely have the budget to test both the online and offline paths.
- Source: [S3]
- Quote: "Teams of two to six people rarely have the budget to test both paths on every release."

## C21
- Claim: The essay does not argue for staying on paper.
- Source: [S3]
- Quote: "None of this means small teams should stay on paper."

## C22
- Claim: The hard part is agreeing who owns which record before day one.
- Source: [S2]
- Quote: "The hard part is agreeing who owns which record and writing that down before the first day in the field."
