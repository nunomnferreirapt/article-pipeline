# Survey notes: connectivity and tooling on small field crews (2025)

This is an invented source written for the article-pipeline example. The
numbers below are made up and describe no real survey.

## Method

Between March and June 2025 we surveyed 140 small field crews. A crew counted
as small when it had two to six people on site. Crews worked in soil sampling,
utility inspection, forestry plots and shoreline monitoring across six
countries. Each crew lead filled in one questionnaire and 31 of them agreed to
a follow-up call.

## Connectivity

Of the 140 crews surveyed, 87 reported at least one offline hour on a typical
working day. The median crew spent 3 hours a day without a usable connection.
Only 12 crews said they were never offline during working hours.

## Tooling

At the time of the survey, 64 crews used an offline-first app as their main
field tool. An offline-first app, for the purpose of this survey, stores every
record on the device first and syncs when a connection returns.

Crews that moved to offline-first tools cut their end-of-day sync time from 3.5
hours to 40 minutes on average. Before the switch, the most common evening task
was re-entering notes taken on paper during the offline hours.

Of the crews that adopted an offline-first tool in 2024, 62 percent were still
using it a year later. Nineteen crews had abandoned it. The reasons given most
often were sync conflicts between devices, stale reference data, and a lack of
an audit trail that their client would accept.

## What crews said they wanted

The three features crews ranked highest were: work without any connection,
sync without manual steps, and a clear record of who changed what and when.
