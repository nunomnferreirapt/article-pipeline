# Interview transcript: a crew lead on two years with offline-first tools

This is an invented interview written for the article-pipeline example. The
crew lead, the crew and the events are fictional.

**Interviewer:** What did a working day look like before you switched?

**Crew lead:** We would be out in the field from seven until four. For most of
that time there was no signal. So the forms lived on paper and the photos lived
on three different phones. Then at night somebody sat in the van or the hotel
and typed it all in. We lost about two hours every evening to re-entering what
we had already written down once.

**Interviewer:** Was that the reason to switch?

**Crew lead:** Mostly, yes. Nobody on the crew asked for new technology. We
wanted our evenings back. The app has to work in a hole in the ground or it is
not a field tool.

**Interviewer:** How long did it take to get the crew comfortable?

**Crew lead:** About two weeks. The first week was rough because people did not
trust that the data was saved when the screen said offline. By the second week
nobody looked at the signal bars any more.

**Interviewer:** What went wrong?

**Crew lead:** The first time two devices edited the same record we got a
conflict screen that nobody understood. We lost a morning sorting it out. We
also learned the hard way that the reference lists on the device can be a month
old if nobody triggers a sync. Now the rule is that the lead syncs every
device before we leave the depot.

**Interviewer:** Would you go back?

**Crew lead:** No. But I would tell anyone starting out that the app is the
easy part. The hard part is agreeing who owns which record and writing that
down before the first day in the field.
